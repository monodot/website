Fund ledgers

Every buy and sell for each fund in the order HMRC processes them, with the pool after each step. Click a row or a marker to see what it's matched with. Sales and purchases on the same day are combined into one line, as HMRC treats them.

How this is worked out

  • Matching. Each sale is matched first with shares of the same fund bought on the same day, then with any bought in the following 30 days (the bed and breakfasting rule), and only then with the average cost of the Section 104 pool.
  • Costs. Quantity × price, plus brokerage on buys. Brokerage on sales is deducted from proceeds.
  • Allowance. £12,300 up to 2022/23, £6,000 in 2023/24, £3,000 from 2024/25. Same-year losses always come off in full. Losses carried forward only bring gains down to the allowance, never below.
  • Rates. 10% / 20% for disposals before 30 October 2024, 18% / 24% from then on. In 2024/25 the losses and allowance go against the higher-rate gains first.
  • Your income decides how much is taxed at the lower rate: whatever's left of the basic rate band after your taxable income. Leave it blank and everything is taxed at the higher rate. Pension contributions and Gift Aid extend the band; this doesn't model that.
  • Not covered. Accumulation funds: the reinvested income you're taxed on each year (shown on your annual tax voucher) should be added to the cost of the holding, which cuts your gain. Equalisation payments on first-year fund units also reduce cost. Add these as extra "Buy" rows with quantity 0 and the amount as price × 1, or adjust manually.
  • This is an estimate to check your own figures against, not tax advice.
