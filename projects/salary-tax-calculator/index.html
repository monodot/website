Rules applied

  • Personal allowance £12,570. It drops by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000 and is gone at £125,140, which makes the effective rate in that band 60% (62% with NI).
  • Income tax 20% on the first £37,700 of taxable income, 40% up to £125,140, 45% above.
  • Employee NI (Class 1) 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270, 2% above. Worked out on an annual basis; payroll does it per pay period, so a bonus month can differ a little. Benefits in kind don't attract employee NI.
  • Salary sacrifice comes off your pay before tax, NI and student loan are worked out, so you save all three. Your employer also saves 15% employer NI on it.
  • SIPP (relief at source). You pay 80%, the provider claims 20% from HMRC. The gross amount also widens your basic and higher rate bands and reduces adjusted net income, which can restore the personal allowance above £100k. The extra relief over 20% is claimed through self-assessment, shown here as a reclaim.
  • Student loan 9% over the threshold: Plan 1 £26,900, Plan 2 £29,385, Plan 4 £33,795, Plan 5 £25,000. Postgraduate loan 6% over £21,000, on top of any undergraduate plan.
  • Employer NI 15% over £5,000, shown for context. It isn't deducted from your pay.
  • Not covered. Scottish income tax rates, savings interest, dividends, capital gains, self-employed or rental income, the High Income Child Benefit Charge, and tax code adjustments for things like underpayments from earlier years.
  • This is an estimate to check your payslip and self-assessment against, not tax advice.
